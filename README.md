When Does a Boy Actually Stop Growing?
Most parents have a rough idea: “somewhere around 18.” But the real answer is a lot more interesting than a birthday.

What actually decides a boy's final height isn't the calendar — it's his growth plates. These are thin bands of cartilage near the ends of the long bones, and as long as they stay open, there's still room to grow. Once rising testosterone (and the estrogen it converts into) causes them to fuse, height is locked in for good — often years before anyone notices it happening.

Puberty itself doesn't follow a fixed script either. Most boys start somewhere between ages 9 and 14, and somewhere in the middle of that stretch comes peak height velocity — the single fastest growth window of a boy's life, typically landing between 13 and 15, about a year or two later than the equivalent moment for girls. That extra runway before the plates finally close is actually the main reason adult men end up taller than adult women on average.

Some boys are essentially done growing by 16. Others keep adding height well into their early twenties — and understanding why can save a lot of unnecessary worry for parents watching a “late bloomer.”
